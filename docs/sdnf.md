# SDNF (`.sdnf`): the Steel Detailing Neutral File

Decision 474, a tester's report #141: *"SDNF is about the only format that allows round-tripping through supported applications."* Lapin writes SDNF 3.0 and reads 3.0 and 2.0, in the steel module (a product without it offers neither). Tekla Structures, Advance Steel, SDS2, ProStructures, AVEVA E3D, Smart 3D/FrameWorks, RISA, SCIA and Dlubal read and write it. Decision 480 added curved members (packet 60), holes (packet 22) and the bolts' holes.

## The specification

Intergraph defined SDNF for exchanging steel structures between analysis and design programs and steel detailing programs. Its public descriptions, which Lapin follows:

- **Version 3.0, every record** — AVEVA E3D Structural Design User Guide, *SDNF – Structural Steel Detailing Neutral File Format* (https://help.aveva.com/AVEVA_Everything3D/3.1/SDUVPDMS/OSUG3.11.05.html): the packets' record tables (00, 10, 20, 22, 30, 40, 50, 60) and their interpretation: cardinal point line, cutbacks, eccentricities, orientation vector and rotation, plate loops.
- **Version 2.0** — CATIA V5 Structure Design, *Exporting SDNF* (http://catiadoc.free.fr/online/sr1ug_C2/sr1ugat0801.htm): the general rules (ASCII, 132 characters, blank-delimited fields, strings in double quotes, `#` comments, fixed packet headers; field formats FP, I, Cnn) and version 2's packets 00, 10 and 20.
- **The packets as programs use them** — Tekla's *SDNF* page (https://support.tekla.com/article/sdnf, now https://help.trimble.com/article/sdnf: packet 22 *hole elements*, Tekla's polygon cuts written as them; packet 60 *curved members*, its curved beams), SDS2's *Export Model – SDNF* (https://techsupport.sds2.com/main/Topics/mlnk_sdn.htm: version 3.0 adds the version line and packet 22, *"hole, slot and Cut Layout information"*; pre-3.0 files have no packet 22), and Bentley ProStructures' *SDNF* (https://docs.bentley.com/LiveContent/web/ProStructures%20Help-v12/en/PS_SDNF.html: packet 22 *Drill Holes (Hole Element Packet)*, packet 60 *Bent Shapes (Arc Member Packet)*).
- **Packets 22 and 60, record by record** — the AVEVA page above (its record tables are pictures, `images/SDNF_0058.png`–`SDNF_0061.png` and `SDNF_0065.png`–`SDNF_0066.png` beside it). None of the sources has a bolt packet: SDNF carries holes, not bolts.

**The file.** Packets, each a fixed header record (`Packet 10`; `Packet10` is read too) and free-format records:

| Packet | What | Lapin |
|---|---|---|
| 00 | Title: version (3.0 only), engineering firm, client, structure, project, date and time, revision and issue code (the program that wrote it), design code, the load records | written and read |
| 10 | Linear members: the units and their count, then ten records each (six in 2.0) | written and read |
| 20 | Plates: linear and thickness units and their count, then a plate's records | written and read |
| 22 | Holes: linear and thickness units and their count, then a hole's records | written and read (480) |
| 30, 40 | Member loads; connection details (text) | read past, noted |
| 50 | Grids: sets of lines (label, start, end) | written and read |
| 60 | Curved members: the units and their count, then eleven records each | written and read (480) |

**A linear member (packet 10).**
1. Member number, cardinal point, status, class, type, piece mark, revision (`4I, 2C24, I`).
2. Section, grade, rotation, mirror X, mirror Y (`2C24, FP, 2I`).
3. Orientation vector; start; end; start and end cutbacks (`3FP, 3FP, 3FP, 2FP`).
4. Cross-section X and Y offsets (`2FP`).
5. Start and end X, Y, Z offsets (`6FP`): from the member's end to its node.
6. Releases: Tx Ty Tz Rx Ry Rz at each end (`12I`, 1 released).
7. Fabricator, prebuy mark, subtype, created and modified dates and times, updated, approval status.
8. The connections at each end, configuration, assemblies (`12I`).
9. Material, coatings, fireproofing (`2I, FP, 2I, 2FP`).
10. Fabricator's note, five user attributes (`6I`).

A file that writes more records per member (FrameWorks writes eleven) is read by the count: the packet's records divided by its members.

**A plate (packet 20, 3.0).** Number, connect point (0 centred, 1 the positive face, −1 the negative), status, class, type, revision; piece mark, grade, thickness, vertices per face, offset, periphery (0 straight edges, 1 straight and curved, 2 a circle); the top face's loop then the bottom's, each vertex `x y z flag` (1 a straight segment follows, 2 a curved one, 0 on the closing vertex, the first again); a circle is its centre on each face, the flag its radius; then four records like a member's 7–10. Version 2.0: piece mark, grade, thickness, vertex count, and one loop of `x y z`.

**A hole (packet 22).** Hole number and parent plate number (`2I`); periphery, thickness, vertices per face (`I, FP, I`); the top face's loop and the bottom's as a plate's (a circle its centre on each face, the flag its radius; the closing vertex flagged 0); approval status, design status, fabricator's note, hole treatment (`4I`); three user attributes (`3I`). AVEVA makes a hole through its plate an opening and one less deep a recess, and checks that it goes through its plate (*"sometimes, holes have been allocated to the wrong plate"*).

**A curved member (packet 60).** Eleven records: a linear member's ten with a third between its second and its third — the arc's centre, start angle, sweep and the normal of its plane (`8FP`). The start and end coordinates (record 4) are the arc's ends. The descriptions do not say from what the start angle runs nor which way the sweep turns: Lapin reads the sweep counter-clockwise about the normal (right-handed, a negative one the other way) and writes the start angle from the plane's x (the world's x laid into the plane; its y when the plane stands across x). Reading, it takes the arc from the centre and the two ends, the way the sweep and the normal say, so the start angle is not needed.

**Meaning.**
- **Cardinal point**: where the member's line runs through its section's box, 1–9 in rows from the bottom, left to right (5 the middle; 2 bottom centre, 8 top centre); 10 the centroid.
- **Orientation vector**: the section's up (its y, a beam's web) before the **rotation**, a right-handed turn about the member's axis looking from start to end.
- **Mirror**: Lapin reads *mirror Y-axis* as the section mirrored about its Y axis (x → −x, an angle or channel facing the other way) and *mirror X-axis* as mirrored about its X axis (the same turned half round). The descriptions do not say more.
- **Coordinates**: the start and end of the cardinal point's line, the real steel (AVEVA's reading); the cutbacks shorten it; the 3D offsets go from each end to its node.

## Export (File → Export IFC, Fragments or BOS… → **SDNF**; `steel.sdnfExport`)

`engine/src/Modules/Steel/Lapin.Steel/Sdnf/`: `SdnfFile.cs` (the records, the writer and the reader), `SdnfProfiles.cs`, `SdnfExport.cs`, `SdnfImport.cs`. The query takes the dialog's storeys, kinds and what the 3D view hides, and returns `{name, data, report}`.

- **Members**: the structure's columns and beams, of any section and material (SDNF allows any), and the steel module's members. Each line is the axis Lapin builds the body on (`MemberAxes`): a column from its foot up its height, centred (cardinal point 5); a beam along its clear span between the columns' faces, at its top (8), its own ends — the columns' centres — as the offsets to its nodes; a member through its insertion point (centre 5, top 8, bottom 2). The section's up is the orientation vector, the rotation 0; a flipped beam or member is mirrored about its Y axis. A steel end's extension, cut, mitre or fit is the cutback (the body's stations off the axis's ends). The type: `Column`, `Beam`, or the member's predefined type (`Brace`, `Rafter`, `Purlin`…). The grade: the strength class the material's name says (S355), else the material's name.
- **Curved beams** (480): curved members (packet 60), as a straight beam is written: the clear span along the arc at its top (cardinal point 8), the arc's ends the columns' centres as the offsets to its nodes; the centre at the top's height, the normal up (or down, so the sweep turns counter-clockwise about it), the sweep, the start angle; the section's up the orientation vector, mirrored when flipped.
- **Sections**: a library profile by its name (IPE 300, HEA 240, L 100x100x10, a project's AISC or own profile); a section with no name by its kind and sizes in millimetres: `I300x150x7.1x10.7`, `RHS200x100x8`, `CHS114.3x5`, `C200x65x2x20`, `300x400` (a rectangle, width × depth), `D300` (a circle). `×` and `Σ` are written as `x` and `SIGMA` (the file is ASCII).
- **Plates**: the plates of the steel connections (end, base, anchor plates) of the members written: the largest face and the face through the thickness, as two loops; grade from the part (S235). The plates an SDNF file brought, as the file had them (kept with each, `sdnf.def`), under their own numbers.
- **Holes** (480, packet 22): the holes the connections' bolts and anchors make in their plates — each fastener line that crosses both faces of a plate within its grip, inside its outline — round, the connection's rule wider than the bolt (2 mm, 3 mm past M24; `NodeSpec.Hole`), their centres on the two faces; a slotted fin plate's holes are written round (Lapin keeps no slot length). The holes an SDNF file brought into a plate, as the file had them. A member's holes (`MemberHoles`), round or a loop, their parent the member's number: SDNF's parent is a plate's, so another program that finds no plate of that number leaves them out (AVEVA says it checks a hole against its plate); Lapin finds them again. A hole keeps the number it came with.
- **Grids**: each IfcGrid a set, its axes at ±0.00.
- **Units**: millimetres, to a thousandth (fewer decimals only when a record would pass 132 characters); the orientation vector to nine places.
- **Ids**: a member's number is the one it came with from an SDNF file (`sdnf.id`), else one from its GlobalId (FNV-1a, at most eight digits, the next free one on a clash). A comment after the title maps each number to its element: `# Lapin member 37239609 00tMh1vYTFOfUQA5$zsorE`, `# Lapin plate n node:<GlobalId>:<part>`, `# Lapin grid n <GlobalId>`. Comments are allowed anywhere; other programs skip them.
- **Releases**: none (Lapin has no end releases yet): every end is written fixed. Status, class, revision and the records 7–10 are written as zeros (the dates are the export's).

## Import (File → Import SDNF…; `steel.importSdnf {name, data | text | path}`)

One undo step; the report counts what was made, updated and made again, the plates and grids, the profiles no library has, and notes. It is the import's **protocol** (report #148, below).

- **Class**: a member called a column that stands upright (and is not a mirrored handed section) becomes a column; one called a beam (girder, joist) that lies level with its section upright becomes a beam; anything else a steel member, its predefined type from the file's type (BRACE, PURLIN, RAFTER, POST, STRUT…). The section's box centre is found from the cardinal point, the frame from the orientation vector, rotation and mirrors; a column's foot, rotation and own height, a beam's ends, top and top offset, a member's ends, rotation and insertion follow. Storeys: a column's and a member's the highest whose level is at or below its foot, a beam's the one under its top; an element brought in before stays on its own.
- **Sections** (`SdnfProfiles.Resolve`): the project's copies of library profiles by name, then Lapin's catalogue and built-in EN library (spaces, `x` and `×` alike), then the generic names above. Any other name (an AISC `W12X50` when the project has no AISC table) is kept as the type's name on a 100 mm square placeholder, listed in the report, until its type is given a profile.
- **Types and materials**: the project's type of that class with that profile and material, else a new one; the grade a material of that name, or one whose strength class is that grade.
- **Ids**: Lapin's comments give each element's GlobalId; else a member numbered as one the project holds — brought in before from the same source (`sdnf: {id, source}`, the source the title's firm, client, structure and project), or exported by Lapin (its number from its GlobalId) — is that element; else a GlobalId derived from the source and number. Importing a file again therefore updates what it brought: positions, sections, storeys; an element keeps its other attributes (its steel ends). One whose class no longer fits (a column now sloping) is made again under its id as what it now is.
- **Beams from Lapin**: their nodes' offsets put their ends back on the columns' centres; Lapin stops them at the faces again.
- **Cutbacks**: on a new steel element, its ends' extensions (negative).
- **Plates**: plates with their bodies (`IfcPlate`), a prism between the two loops (a version 2 plate from its loop, its thickness and connect point; a circle by the chord rule; curved segments straight between their vertices). The plates of a Lapin connection still in the project are left to the connection, which builds them (and their holes). A plate Lapin wrote with its GlobalId in the comment comes back under it.
- **Curved members** (480): one lying level (its plane's normal upright, its ends and centre at one height), its section upright all along, becomes a curved beam: the cardinal point's line moved to the section's top centre (across the arc a smaller or larger radius, up or down), the arc through its ends about the file's centre the way the sweep turns (the beam's rise), its storey under its top. Lapin's own file's node offsets put its ends back on the columns' centres. One arching in another plane, or with its section turned, is left out and said: Lapin's curved members are level beams. A curved member nearly a full circle is left out too.
- **Holes** (480): a hole whose parent is a plate in the file is cut in that plate's body (`Prism` with holes: the hole's top loop, or its circle by the chord rule, laid onto the plate's face along its thickness), when it goes through it (as deep as the plate, or no depth given) and lies inside it; a recess and a hole off its plate are said and left out. A hole whose parent no plate has and a member has (as Lapin writes them) is kept on the member (`holes`: each in the member's frame — the section's x and y and the station from the member's own start, a beam's own start at its top — so it moves with it), taken off its body by the boolean library and written to IFC as an opening of its swept body. Curved edges come straight between their vertices, said. A file read again puts the holes it has in place of an element's, so they are never doubled, and an element whose holes the file no longer has keeps none. A hole naming nothing in the file is said.
- **Bolts**: SDNF carries none (no packet for them in any description), so the holes come in without them and the report says so; Lapin's bolts stay those of its own steel connections.
- **Grids**: an IfcGrid per set, updated by its id.
- **Left out**, in notes: loads (Lapin keeps none), connection details, end releases.

### Large files, progress and the protocol (report #148, decision 569)

A tester's report #148: *"I tried to load a file which is quite large and found myself back on the normal application desktop. Only now it was very slow to react to clicks … I waited a while but did not find anything from the file. Neither was there a protocol."* The window called the import with no overlay, so it stayed on the desktop while the engine worked, every other call queued behind it (the slow clicks), and the import took minutes, growing with the square of the frame, past the call's ten-minute wait on a slower computer; the timeout's notice was easy to miss and nothing said what the file held.

- **Speed.** Measured on a generated frame of 11,096 members (25 × 25 bays, six storeys), 625 base plates and 2,500 holes (136,000 lines), in the Debug engine: **256 s before, 23 s after**. Three causes, each quadratic: (1) every member's storey and type was related one at a time, and a relationship of thousands is copied and indexed again at each (30.8 s of the 35.5 s the command took): the import now collects them and relates each storey's and type's in one go (`EditRelations.RelateAll`), then sets the members' holes; (2) a beam's span looked at every column of the project (`Beams.Solve`) and (3) its cut by higher-ranked elements read every possible cutter (`ModuleCatalog.For`), so each of 7,000 beams read and noted 7,500 columns' keys (building 7,000 beams and then registering 52 million dependencies took 210 s): past `NearReads.WholeUpTo` (500) columns or cutters of a class, both now read only what is round the element, by the cells in plan of `NearReads` (a column found by its point within `Beams.ColumnReach`, 5 m, of the axis; a cutter by its envelope's box, the cutters of a revision indexed by cell once). A column or cutter elsewhere that comes near, or leaves, is built again where it was and is, which touches the cells the beam read, so the beam is still built again then. Up to 500 a class is read whole, as before: the dependencies stay exact and an undo finds the earlier builds again (`EditingSpeedTests`); past it a cell has no stamp, so such a build is made again on an undo rather than found.
- **Progress.** The import tells its stages as it goes (`ctx.Query.Progress`: *Reading the file*, *Bringing the members in* n / N, *Bringing the plates in*, *Placing the holes*, then the session's *Building the model*), shown by the busy overlay as an IFC import is, the time running.
- **Cancel.** The overlay's **Cancel** sends `cancel.long`; the dispatcher gives every call a token (`Session.Cancellation`, `IQueryContext.Cancellation`), the import looks at it at each item and stops, its edit undone (*Cancelled: nothing of the file was imported.*). The bodies built after the command are not stopped (the button goes then), nor the engine in a browser page (it works the call in one go: no Cancel there).
- **Errors.** A record that cannot be read stops the reading with its line, packet and item (`SdnfFormatException`): *Line 1234, packet 10, member 57 of 3000: its start's x is „12,5”, not a number.* The coordinates (a member's orientation, start and end, a curved member's centre and sweep, a plate's thickness, every vertex, a grid line's ends) must be numbers; a packet that ends before its last record says where. Nothing of the file comes in. A member or plate that throws while it is made is left out and listed with the exception's message, the rest coming in.
- **The protocol.** The report adds: `packets` (each packet's number, header line, the items its count announces, those read, those imported; a packet holding fewer than it announces also said in the notes), `skips` and `warnings` (what was left out and what was changed, grouped by reason, each with its count and up to 200 items, a member's number and line: *members shorter than a millimetre*, *members in the file twice*, *curved members arching out of a level plane*, *recesses*, *holes not inside their plate*, *holes naming a plate or member the file does not have*…; a line for each group in `notes`, ten items shown), `lines` and `ms` (reading and the model; the window adds the bodies' building). When the import ends the window shows it (Import SDNF → the **SDNF import** dialog: the headline, the time, the packets' table, what was left out, what changed, the placeholder profiles, the notes), with **Copy** (the protocol as text, every item kept) and **Save…** (`<file>-import.txt`); a file that could not be read opens the same dialog with why and where. `ui/src/modules/steel/sdnfReport.ts` lays the text out.

## Tests

`SdnfTests`: the file's packet layout (the title's ten records, ten records a member of 7, 5, 11, 2, 6, 12, 9, 12, 7 and 6 fields, the plates' and grids' headers, 132 characters, ASCII); a frame exported and imported into an empty project (classes, profiles, ends, frames, insertion and bodies to 0.1 mm, the beam back on its nodes, the grid, one undo step); a re-import into the same project updating every element and nothing added, a sloping column made again as a member; another program's 3.0 file in feet and inches (a W12X50 kept as a placeholder, cardinal point 1, a plate) imported twice, and a 2.0 file; a hall's plates out and back. Decision 480: curved beams (one between columns, one free) out as packet 60 (eleven records, the arc's eight fields) and back on the same arcs to 0.1 mm, read again as updates; another program's level curved member on cardinal point 5 as a curved beam, a vertical one said; a plate's round and rectangular holes and a beam's web hole cut in their bodies (volumes), out with their numbers and back in the same places, a recess and a hole of no plate said, the IFC opening; a file read again replacing the holes, one without the beam's hole leaving its web whole, one undo step; a hall's bolt and anchor holes in its plates (the hole sizes), back as holes with the no-bolts note. `SdnfLargeTests` (report #148): a generated frame of 3,537 members, 400 plates and 1,600 holes in under a minute (seconds on a laptop), every one in, one undo step, a beam's columns read a handful, the stages told; the protocol's packets (announced, read, imported, the header's line), the groups with their items' numbers and lines (a member twice, one shorter than a millimetre, a hole naming no plate), a packet cut short, loads, packet 70 and a placeholder profile said; a malformed coordinate and a plate packet ending early told with their lines and nothing imported; a cancelled import leaving the project as it was. `sdnfReport.test.ts`: the protocol's text.

## Not done

- End releases (Lapin has none), loads.
- Curved members out of a level plane (arched rafters): Lapin's curved members are level beams.
- Recesses (holes less deep than their plate); slots by their length (a slot comes as its outline; Lapin's connections' slotted holes go out round); the holes the bolts make in members' flanges and webs (only plates' are written).
- Bolts: no SDNF packet carries them.
- Profiles named by another program's table (W12X50) resolve only through the project's own profiles: a mapping table of names is for later.
