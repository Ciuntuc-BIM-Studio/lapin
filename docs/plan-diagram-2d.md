# The diagram look in 2D views: plan

Forum topic 283 (a user request, 26.11.2026). The 3D view has a **Diagram** display (decision 843: View → Display quality → Diagram, `ui/src/viewport/diagram.ts`): a white model, or a muted pastel by category, with thin light edges. The user asks for the same look in plans, elevations and sections: soft fills for what is cut and what is seen, clean thin outlines, white or pastel by category, chosen **per view** (a plan can stay a technical drawing while an elevation uses the diagram look), so presentations and client documents match the 3D. Decision 885 (the register's last row on origin/main was 882; 883 and 884 may be taken by branches in flight).

## 1. What is there

- **The 3D diagram** is a look laid over the elements' own, on screen only. `categoryOf(type, predefinedType)` maps an IFC class to eight categories (walls, floors, roofs, structure, openings, site, greenery, other; spaces, opening elements, annotations, grids and virtual elements are skipped; a geographic element is greenery when its predefined type says vegetation). `diagramColour` gives the white model's one colour (`#ecebe8`) or the category's pastel (`DEFAULT_PALETTE`, with the user's changes over it). A colour an element already has (Visibility/Graphics, a phase, 4D) wins. The variant and the palette are kept on the computer (`localStorage` `lapin.diagram`), edited in Preferences → Display → Diagram colours.
- **A 2D view's look** is one path for the screen, sheets, PDF, SVG and DXF: `Views.Render` → `MaterialDrawing.Apply(drawing, ViewMaterialGraphics.Band, …)` (each cut band's fill, hatch and lines) → `Pens.Of` (fills, hatches, lines; Visibility/Graphics' category fill, cut hatch, line colour and weight, and the elements' colours from filters, phases and halftone laid over the materials') → `SurfaceHatches.PensOf` (an elevation's or a section's seen faces, filled and hatched with their materials' elevation graphics, where nothing nearer hides them) and `SurfaceHatches.PlanPensOf` (a plan's floors seen below the cut, hatch lines only).
- **The view style** (`ViewStyles.Style`, docs/view-styles.md) holds the per-view-kind switches (`surfaceHatch`, `floorHatch`, `finishHatch`, `smallScaleCut`, `vegetation`…); a view takes its style's and sets any field of its own (`styleOverrides`, `view.setStyle`, one undo step), edited in View → View settings and Manage → View styles. `ViewStyles.Of` resolves it for every consumer.
- **`ViewMaterialGraphics`** is the one precedence for how a material draws in a view: the element's own override, the view's filters, the view's subcategory graphics, the material graphics set, the small-scale cut, the layer's graphics, the material's. The small-scale cut's `solid` mode already turns each cut element into one area of one colour, its bands merged, no hatch: the diagram's cut is the same mechanism with the palette's colour.

## 2. The design

- **A field of the view style, `diagram`**: `off` (the default; a style or a view saved before reads so, and nothing is written for it, so no stored format changes and no `IProjectUpgrade` is needed), `white` or `pastel`. A style sets it for its views, a view sets its own over its style (`view.setStyle {id, diagram}`, `viewStyle.update {id, diagram}`), so a plan can be technical while an elevation is a diagram. It is in View → View settings (plans, sections, elevations) and in Manage → View styles (a **Diagram** column).
- **One palette, the project's.** The drawings are the engine's, and a sheet must print the same on any computer, so the palette moves from the computer to the project: `diagramPalette` on the project (only the categories that differ from the default; `diagram.palette` reads it, `diagram.setPalette {palette: {category: colour | null}, reset?}` changes it, one undo step). The 3D diagram reads the same palette, so 3D and drawings stay in step. Preferences → Display → Diagram colours edits the open project's palette. The coded default stays the same (`DEFAULT_PALETTE` in the UI, `DiagramLook.Defaults` in the engine; a test reads both and checks they are equal), and so does the category mapping (`DiagramLook.CategoryOf`, the same classes as `categoryOf`). A palette changed on a computer before this (two days of decision 843) keeps showing in the 3D of a project that has none of its own, until the palette is edited once in Preferences: then the project takes it, with the change. The variant of the 3D display (white or pastel) stays the computer's, as a display choice; a view's 2D variant is the view's own.
- **What the style does, in the drawing the engine makes** (so the screen, sheets, PDF, SVG and DXF alike):
  - **Cut areas**: each cut element of a category one area filled with its category's colour (white: `#ecebe8`), its layers merged, no hatch, no membrane line, no lines between its layers: a tier of `ViewMaterialGraphics` over the material graphics set and under the view's own Visibility/Graphics (its subcategories, filters, elements' overrides), so what the view sets explicitly wins. A category's fill colour or cut hatch in Visibility/Graphics, a filter's or a phase's colour (paled, as today) win over the palette, as they do in 3D.
  - **Seen surfaces**: an elevation's and a section's faces seen beyond the cut are filled with their element's category colour (under the lines, where nothing nearer hides them), and a plan's floors, terrain and other category faces seen from above below the cut likewise. A Visibility/Graphics category fill, a filter's or an element's surface fill, or an element's colour wins.
  - **Hatches off**: the surface hatches, the floors' surface hatches and the rooms' floor finish hatches are off in a diagram view, unless the view itself turns them on (its own `surfaceHatch`, `floorHatch`, `finishHatch`); the fills replace them.
  - **Thin, light outlines**: cut lines 0.25 mm in a mid grey (`#7a8087`), seen lines 0.13 mm in a light grey (`#a4a9af`); hidden, overhead and beyond lines as before. Visibility/Graphics' line colour or weight for a category, a line edit (Linework) and an element's colour still win; the project's Object styles do not (they are the technical drawing's pens).
  - What has no category (rooms, opening voids, annotations, grids) and the annotations, dimensions and tags are drawn as before.
- **Unchanged**: a view whose style and own settings say `off` draws exactly as before (a test compares its pens byte for byte); the 3D diagram's look; the model, quantities and IFC.

## 3. Stages

1. **This branch.** The `diagram` field (engine, `view.setStyle`, `viewStyle.update`, `view.style`), the project palette (`diagram.palette`, `diagram.setPalette`), the drawing (cuts, seen surfaces in elevations, sections and plans, hatches off, light lines), the pickers in View settings and Manage → View styles, the 3D reading the project's palette and Preferences editing it, every language; xUnit `DiagramViewTests`, vitest for the palette's source; the guide (views.md, en and ro).
2. **Later, if asked.** The view's background on screen in the diagram's light grey (`#f4f4f2`, the 3D's); a sheet's viewports in a diagram style showing a matching key (a legend of the categories' colours, from `DrawingKeys`); soft sun shadows in elevations on by default in a diagram view (they exist, `ViewShadows`, and are the view's own switch today); a per-view palette (a style naming its own palette) if one project needs two.

## 4. Open questions for the user

1. **Line colours and weights**: 0.25 mm mid grey for the cut, 0.13 mm light grey for what is seen. Darker or lighter? Should the cut outline stay black, only thinner?
2. **The palette on the project** (not the computer) is the choice taken so sheets print alike everywhere and the 3D matches them. Is a project palette right, or should a palette also be kept on the computer as the default for new projects?
3. **Shadows**: should a diagram elevation turn the sun's shadows on by itself (as the 3D diagram has them), or leave that to the view's own setting (today's choice)?
4. **Doors and windows** in plans take the openings' colour on their cut parts (frames, glass): enough, or should their symbols (swings) stay as they are and the frames white?
